Dual-Source Semantic Enhancement via Reliable Semantic Approximation for Sparse Text Supervision

Hongyu Liu, Yunfei Yin, Swachha Ray, Sijing Xiong and Argho Dey

Pattern Recognition, under review.

Abstract

Language-guided medical image segmentation leverages clinical reports as semantic priors to improve lesion localisation and representation learning, but paired image-text annotations are expensive, so most training images have no corresponding report. Existing methods construct semantic approximations from limited paired samples, and these suffer from two limitations: semantic unreliability, where approximated semantics derived from visually similar samples may not describe the current image, and semantic incompleteness, where sparse annotations leave some regions without adequate coverage. We propose a Dual-Source Semantic Enhancement Framework (DSEF) that integrates semantic approximations with learnable visual prototypes. A cross-modal semantic localisation module estimates visual-text correspondence to filter unreliable semantics and identify incomplete regions; a prototype semantic compensation module fills those regions with visual prototypes; and a staged prototype evolution strategy with orthogonality regularisation improves prototype diversity and stability. On QaTa-COV19 and MosMedData+, DSEF is effective across text-supervision ratios; with only 1% text supervision on QaTa-COV19 it reaches a Dice score of 0.8781, surpassing the best competing method by 1.73%.
