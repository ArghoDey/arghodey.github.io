Flexible Dilated Convolution and Bidirectional Pyramid Approach to Polyp Segmentation

Yunfei Yin, Zumnan Timbi, Zheng Yuan, Sijing Xiong, Md Tanvir Islam, Argho Dey and Swachha Ray

Biomedical Signal Processing and Control, 2025.

PaperCode

Abstract

Polyp segmentation is a crucial step for polypectomy, and irregular, small and weak boundaries remain a significant challenge. State-of-the-art methods employ multi-scale context dilated convolution, but their performance is constrained by fixed dilation rates. We present a Convolution Enhanced Bidirectional Pyramid Boundary Refinement (CEBPBR) framework built from three blocks: a bidirectional pyramid feature extractor with enhanced depth-wise separable convolution and learnable attention pooling, which uses a bottom-up stream with progressively larger dilations to collect coarse context, a top-down mirror that pulls the dilations down, and lateral connections that re-inject high-resolution information; an auxiliary boundary head that preserves contours and edge features at every pyramid layer; and a learnable boundary refinement module that fuses edge features with condensed high-level semantics through a gating mechanism. On public polyp datasets, CEBPBR outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 1.5% in mDice, 3.3% in mIoU and 48% in MAE, with visibly better boundaries.
